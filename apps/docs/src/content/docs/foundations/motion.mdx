Motion in Tanrei explains a change and then gets out of the way. Most of it is a state layer fading in over 150 milliseconds.

## Two kinds of movement

Motion tokens are organised as springs along two axes:

- **Spatial** springs move or resize things. They may overshoot slightly, the way a physical object settles.
- **Effects** springs change color and opacity. They never overshoot, because a color that bounces looks like a glitch.

Each kind comes in **fast**, **default** and **slow**. Platforms with real spring animation use the stiffness and damping values; CSS uses the duration and the cubic-bezier approximation stored alongside them.

There are two schemes. **Standard** is the default and is nearly critically damped. **Expressive** is an additive layer with more bounce for products that want it.

## Durations and easing

Fixed durations and easing curves are provided for transitions that are not springs.

| Kind of change | Guideline |
| --- | --- |
| State change (hover, press, selection) | 100–200ms |
| An element entering | about 300ms |
| A large reveal | up to 800ms, with items staggered 100ms apart |
| Anything leaving | faster than it entered |

## What does not move

- **The focus ring appears instantly.** Keyboard users need to see where they are without waiting for an animation.
- **No ripple by default.** Press feedback is the state layer; ripple is a theme-level option, not a baseline.

## Reduced motion

Every implementation must honour the platform's reduced-motion setting. Under it, spatial movement is removed or replaced by a short cross-fade, and nothing loops. This is part of each component's definition of done, not an enhancement.

All values are listed in the [motion token reference](/tokens/motion/).
