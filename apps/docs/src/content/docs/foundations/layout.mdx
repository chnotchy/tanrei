## Window size classes

Tanrei describes available width with five classes. They refer to the window or the container a layout lives in, not to a kind of device: a tablet in split view is compact, and a phone unfolded is not.

| Class | Width | Typical layout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compact | below 600 | One column. Navigation in a bar at the bottom or behind a menu. |
| Medium | 600–839 | One column with wider margins, or two where content allows. A collapsed navigation drawer (a column of icons) becomes possible. |
| Expanded | 840–1199 | Two panes, such as list and detail. Persistent navigation. |
| Large | 1200–1599 | Two or three panes. Line length, not the window, limits text width. |
| Extra-large | 1600 and above | As large, with generous margins; do not stretch content to fill. |

Widths are in density-independent units: CSS pixels on the web, points on Apple platforms, dp on Android, logical pixels in Flutter.

## Adapting between classes

When a layout crosses a boundary, change it in one of a few predictable ways:

- **Reveal** a pane that was behind a tap, such as a detail view beside its list.
- **Divide** one column into two.
- **Resize** panes and margins proportionally.
- **Reposition** an element, for example a navigation bar at the bottom moving to a collapsed navigation drawer at the side.
- **Swap** a component for its larger-screen counterpart, such as a bottom sheet becoming a side sheet.

Three arrangements cover most screens: a **feed** of cards in a responsive grid, **list–detail**, and content with a **supporting pane**.

## Spacing

Spacing tokens are multiples and fractions of an 8-unit base. The number in a token's name is a percentage of that base, so `space100` is 8, `space50` is 4 and `space200` is 16.

Use the small steps inside components, the middle of the scale between components, and the large steps between sections. Spacing between touch targets is never less than `space100`.

## Text width

Keep running text between roughly 45 and 80 characters per line. On large and extra-large windows, cap the text column and let margins or a supporting pane absorb the rest.
