An avatar shows who something belongs to: the author of a post, the people in a conversation, the owner of a task. It draws a picture, and it always has something to draw when there is none. It never answers a pointer, a key or focus.

On the web it is `<tanrei-avatar>`, and several together are `<tanrei-avatar-group>`. The other platforms have not implemented it yet.

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use an avatar where a small picture tells people who or what something belongs to faster than a name alone: a byline, a comment, a row of a list of people, the head of a profile. It is a picture and nothing more. It has no hover, no focus, no press and no event.

| You need | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A dot or a count on a person ("3 unread") | A [badge](/components/badge/) around the avatar. |
| An avatar that leads to a profile | A [link](/components/link/) that holds the avatar. |
| An avatar that opens a menu | A [button](/components/button/) that holds the avatar. |
| A person who can be chosen or removed | A [chip](/components/chip/). |
| Presence (online, away) | Words beside the name, with a [tag](/components/tag/) where it needs a mark. |

### What it draws

The avatar draws the first of these that it has:

1. The picture of `src`, once it has loaded.
2. The `initials` you give.
3. The glyph in its `icon` slot.
4. The system's own glyph: a person for a circle, a neutral mark for a square.

The box keeps its size from the first paint. While a picture loads, the fallback is drawn in its place, and when the picture arrives it fades in as the fallback fades out. A picture the browser already holds is drawn at once. Nothing around the avatar moves. A picture that cannot be loaded leaves the fallback: there is no broken-picture mark, and the avatar does not ask again.

### Initials

The avatar does not make initials from the name. Which characters stand for a name depends on its writing system and on which part comes first, and your product already knows that. Give them in `initials`:

| Name | `initials` |
| --- | --- |
| Ada Lovelace | `AL` |
| 山田 花子 | `山` or `山田` |
| 王小明 | `王` |
| ليلى حسن | `لح` |
| किरण राव | `किरा` |

The avatar draws at most two characters, counted the way a reader counts them, so a letter with its marks or a joined syllable is never cut in half. It draws them as written and does not change their case.

### Sizes and shapes

| `size` | Side | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `small` | 24 | Beside one line of text: a byline, a table cell, a mention. |
| `medium` (default) | 40 | A row of a list, a comment. |
| `large` | 56 | The head of a person's own page or card. |

| `shape` | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| `circle` (default) | A person. |
| `square` | Something that is not one person: a team, a project, an organisation, a bot. |

Every fallback has the same quiet fill. There is no color per person: a color that means nothing is not in the system.

### The picture

- Give a square picture of at least twice the avatar's side, so it stays sharp on a dense screen. A picture of other proportions is cropped around its centre.
- `loading="lazy"` waits to ask for the picture until the avatar is near the window. Use it in long lists.
- `referrerpolicy` and `crossorigin` are passed to the picture as you write them. The avatar chooses neither: what a picture's host is told about your page is your decision.

### Groups

A group draws its members as one row. Each lies a little over the one before it (2, 4 or 8 by size), so two initials side by side stay whole and are not misread, with a gap in the surface's color around each.

<DemoFrame label="A group that counts what it was not given" plain>
  <tanrei-avatar-group label="Members" max="4" total="240" overflow-label="and {count} more">
    <tanrei-avatar label="Ada Lovelace" initials="AL"></tanrei-avatar>
    <tanrei-avatar label="Grace Hopper"></tanrei-avatar>
    <tanrei-avatar label="Katherine Johnson" initials="KJ"></tanrei-avatar>
  </tanrei-avatar-group>
</DemoFrame>

- `max` is the most boxes the group draws, the overflow box included. Six members with `max="4"` are three members and "+3". The group never draws "+1": it would spend a box to hide one face.
- `total` is the real count where you give the group fewer members than exist. Three members, `max="4"` and `total="240"` are three members and "99+": above 99 the box reads "99+" at every size, so it never holds more than three characters. Give the exact number in `overflow-label`, which is what is read.
- The group gives its `size` and `shape` to every member.
- On another surface than the page's, set `--tanrei-comp-avatar-group-outline-color` to that surface's color.
- The overflow box is text, not a control. Where people need to see who else there is, put a link or a button beside the group.

### Before the script arrives

`<tanrei-avatar>` draws in its shadow root, so a page rendered on a server shows nothing in its place until the script runs. Load `@tanrei/web/avatar.css` and each avatar keeps a box of its size and shape, so the page does not move. No picture, initials or name show until then. For a page that must read without script, write the name as text beside a decorative avatar.

A group with `max` shows every member you gave it until the script counts them. If that must not move the page, give the group only the members it draws, and the rest as `total`.

### Do

- Give every avatar that stands alone a `label`: the name in full.
- Set `decorative` on an avatar that stands beside the same name in text.
- Give `initials` to every person, so a missing or slow picture still shows who it is.
- Keep one shape for one kind of thing across the product.

### Don't

- Don't put a handler on an avatar. Put it in a link or a button, which has the states and the focus indicator.
- Don't write "Photo of" in the label. The label is the name.
- Don't use a color, a ring or a dot of your own to show status. A count is a badge, and presence is said in words.
- Don't use an avatar for a decorative picture or a logo in a heading. It stands for someone or something people act as.

## Accessibility

<ComponentAccessibility name="avatar" />

### What the component handles

- Alone, it is one image named by `label`, whether it draws a picture, initials or a glyph. The initials, the glyph's name and the picture's address are never read.
- With `decorative`, it is hidden from assistive technology.
- Inside a link or a button, its label becomes that element's name, and a press on the avatar reaches that element.
- A badge around it adds the badge's label after the avatar's name.
- A group is one group named by its `label`. It holds one named image for each member it draws, in the order given, and then the overflow's text. Members it does not draw are not exposed.
- It takes no input and no focus, so it adds no stop to the focus order.
- With enlarged text it keeps its box, as an icon does. Its initials grow with the text until they fill the glyph's box of the avatar's size, and stay whole inside it.
- In forced-colors mode every fallback gains an edge in the text color. A picture keeps its own colors.
- In a right-to-left layout a group starts at the right. A picture and initials are never mirrored, and the plus sign of "+3" stays before the digits.
- An avatar without a name, initials longer than two characters, a picture that fails, and an unknown size or shape are reported in debug builds.

### What you need to do

- Decide for each avatar whether it is named or decorative. The rule: if deleting the avatar loses a name, it is named. Otherwise it is decorative.
- Write `overflowLabel` once, in the page's language, with the place `{count}` where the number goes (`and {count} more`) — on the group, or for the whole product with `setWords({ avatarGroupOverflowLabel })`. The group fills in the number it does not draw, in full even where it draws 99+. Write a sentence that is right for every number, or set it per count where your language needs another form for one. Without it, "+3" is read as drawn.
- Give a small avatar inside a link enough room to press. The avatar is 24 wide; the link must meet the minimum target itself.
- A picture brings its own colors. Where pictures are often pale at their edge, give the surface behind them a tone.
