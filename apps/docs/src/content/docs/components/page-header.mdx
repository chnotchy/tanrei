A page header opens a page's content. It holds the page's title, one or two sentences that say what the page is, a line of short facts, the actions for the whole page, and the way back up above it. Every part but the title is optional.

It is not an app bar. An app bar is the window's bar: it stays at the top edge and holds the product's name and icon buttons. A page header is in the content, scrolls away with it, and holds labelled Buttons.

On the web it is `<tanrei-page-header>` around the page's own elements. The element has no shadow root. The title is your own `h1`: a crawler, a reader mode and a page without script all find it, and nothing moves when the script arrives. The other platforms have not implemented it yet.

(The examples on this page use a heading of level 3, because this page already has its own title.)

### What you write

Write the parts in the order they are read. That order is also the focus order, at every width.

| Part | On the web |
| --- | --- |
| Breadcrumbs | `<tanrei-breadcrumbs>`, or `<div data-part="breadcrumbs">` around one Link back or your own trail. |
| Eyebrow | `<p data-part="eyebrow">`. Write ordinary words; the capitals are drawn. |
| Title | The heading: `h1` for a page, `h2` for a part of one, or the level your outline needs. |
| Description | The first `<p>` without a `data-part`. |
| Meta | `<div data-part="meta">`. Each element child is one item: a `<time>`, a `<span>`, a Link, a Tag. |
| Actions | `<div data-part="actions">` around your Buttons and Links. |

### A head built by script

`headline`, `description` and `eyebrow` write their elements for you, where the children have none. `level` sets the heading's level; 0 (default) takes it from the size.

```html
<tanrei-page-header headline="Members" description="People who can open this project."></tanrei-page-header>
```

This form is drawn only once the script has run. Write the elements yourself on a content page.

## Guidelines

### Three sizes

| `size` | Title | Description | Use for | Heading level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `small` | 24 | 14 | A part of a page: a section of settings, one record among several. | 2 |
| `medium` (default) | 32 | 16 | A page. | 1 |
| `large` | 48 | 16 | A landing page's statement. | 1 |

A large head in a narrow place draws the medium title. The change is one step, not a size that follows the width.

### The title

- The title is the page's heading. It is shown once, as text, and is never cut, shortened to a number of lines or shrunk to fit. A long title costs height, not words.
- A page has one heading of level 1, and it is this title. Use `small` heads, or headings in the content, for the parts of the page.
- Do not show the same title in the app bar and in the page header. Choose one per window class.

### Eyebrow and breadcrumbs

- Breadcrumbs lead back up through a hierarchy. They take the first place, across the head's whole width.
- The eyebrow says what the page belongs to: a category, a product, a version. It is text, not a link and not a heading.
- A head may have both.

### The meta line

- An item is a short fact that needs no name: a date, an author, a reading time, a state.
- The page header draws a small dot between two items on one row. Do not write a separator yourself.
- An item stays whole on its row. A row that is full wraps.
- An author is one item: a small decorative avatar and the name.
- Several tags are one item that holds the group, so no dot is drawn between two tags.
- Facts with names ("Category: Actions") are a [description list](/components/description-list/) under the head, not meta items.

### Actions

| `actionsPlacement` | While the head is at least 480 wide | While it is narrower |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `end` (default) | Beside the title at the end edge, centred on the title's first line. One row of actions stays centred there, even when it is taller than the line. Actions that wrap onto a second row start at the line's top and the head grows to hold them. | Below the text, at the start edge. |
| `below` | Below the text. | Below the text. |

- The width that decides is the head's own, not the window's. A head in a pane lays itself out for the pane.
- The actions always come last in reading and focus order, wherever they are drawn.
- Use one to three actions, the main one filled.

### The line under it

`divider` draws one hairline under the head. Use it above content that is not text, such as a table, a list of records or tabs. Above an article it is not needed.

### Do

- Write the description as one or two sentences. Longer text belongs to the content.
- Give a date a `<time>` element with a machine-readable `datetime`.
- Set `lang` on text in another language. In Chinese, Japanese and Korean the title is drawn without its negative letter-spacing (the eyebrow keeps its positive one), and in scripts that join their letters or stack marks on them (Arabic, Devanagari) the title and the eyebrow are drawn with a letter-spacing of zero: a word falls apart there when its letters are moved apart or together.

### Don't

- Don't use a page header as the window's bar. That is an [app bar](/components/app-bar/).
- Don't write the eyebrow in capitals. Some screen readers spell capitals letter by letter.
- Don't tell a state by color alone. Use a tag whose label says it.

## Accessibility

<ComponentAccessibility name="page-header" />

### What the component handles

- The title stays the heading you wrote, at its level. Its name is the title's text alone.
- The eyebrow is read before the heading and the description after it. Neither is part of the heading.
- The dots between meta items are not read and not copied.
- The page header adds no landmark and is not a stop of the keyboard.
- The order of reading and of focus is the same at every width.
- With text at 200% in a place 320 wide every word, item and action is shown, and nothing scrolls sideways.
- In forced colors the text, the dots and the line are drawn in the system's text color.
- In a right-to-left layout every part starts at the right and actions beside the text stand at the left. A left-to-right title keeps its own order.

### What you need to do

- One heading of level 1 per page.
- The document's title should be, or begin with, the page's title.
- Names for your actions, and a text alternative for any picture you place in the head.
