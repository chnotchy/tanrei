A list is a box. What each item shows is your own content: text, a picture, a card, a [file item](/components/file-item/), or a [list row](/components/list-row/) — the row of a headline, a line or two of supporting text, and something before and after them that most lists are made of. The list owns everything around the items: how they are arranged, the space and the lines between them, what they are to assistive technology, how keyboard focus moves among them, which of them are chosen, and how people put them in another order.

### Usage

A list has one kind of item, `list-item`, and each item holds your content as its children. `orientation` lays the items out as a column (`vertical`, the default) or a line (`horizontal`); `wrap` lets them run onto further lines, so that they form a grid; `gap` sets the space between them (`none`, `small`, `medium`, `large`), and `dividers` draws a hairline between them in a list that does not wrap.

An item is static unless you make it more. A static item draws nothing and adds no space: its content is the item, and it may hold your own controls. `interactive` makes the item one button; `href` makes it one link, which the browser treats as any link (it can be opened in a new tab and its address copied; `target="new"` opens a new tab and marks the item with `open_in_new`, and `targetLabel` says so). A list whose `selection` is `single` or `multiple` is a listbox, and its items are options chosen by their `value`. An interactive item, a link or an option pads its content, carries every state, and is named by `label` or else by the text of its content.

`reorderable` lets people put the items in another order, in a column, a line or a grid. Every item draws a handle at its start, the sign that it can be moved. The whole item can be dragged, not only the handle: a mouse picks it up once it moves a few pixels, and a finger or a pen after a long press, so that a swipe still scrolls the page (a touch on the handle drags at once). The other items slide out of the way, the item follows the pointer above them, and it settles into its place when it is dropped.

Dragging is never the only way. The handle is a button: pressing it, or Enter, opens a menu of four moves (to the start, one place before, one place after, to the end). With the keyboard, Space on the handle lifts the item, the arrow keys carry it one place at a time (Home and End to the first and the last place), Space or Enter drops it, and Escape puts it back. Each step is announced.

The order is your data, so the list never changes it. Every way of moving fires `move` with `from`, the item's index, and `to`, the index it is to have, both counted from 0. Reorder your items in answer. The list then draws them changing place, keeps focus on the moved item's handle, and tells assistive technology where it now stands. If you change nothing within the time of `comp.list.motion.reorder-duration`, the move was refused: the items go back and nothing is announced.

The words are yours: the handle's name (`dragHandleLabel`, with the place `{label}` the list fills with the item's name), its description (`dragHandleHint`), the four moves of the menu, and the three sentences that are announced (`liftedText`, `movedText`, `cancelledText`, with the places `{label}`, `{position}` and `{count}`). Give them on the list, or once for the product with `setWords()`. In a line, write "Move left" and "Move right" where a column says "Move up" and "Move down".

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a list for a set of things of one kind that people scan, open, choose from or arrange: messages, people, files, photos, settings, the choices of a picker. Use a table when people compare values across columns, and a menu for a short set of actions that opens from a button.

The list draws nothing of its own: no background, no padding around its items and no scrolling. Put it on a surface, in a card, or in your screen's scroll view, and give each item the look it needs in its content.

### The four kinds of item

| Kind | How you get it | What it is | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Static | `selection` none, `interactive` false, no `href` | Your content as it is. No focus, no hover or press. | Items people read, and items that hold their own controls. |
| Interactive | `interactive` | One button inside its list item. | Items that each change something or open it in place. |
| Link | `href` | One link inside its list item, drawn exactly as an interactive item. Enter follows it and Space does not. | Items that each go to a page with an address of its own. |
| Option | `selection` single or multiple | An option of a listbox, with a selected state and a check. | Choosing from the items. |

A list may mix static, interactive and link items. A selectable list holds only options. A reorderable list holds only static items, and is never a listbox: an action, a link or an option is taken by a press that a drag would take.

### Lists people reorder

- Use `reorderable` where the order means something and is the person's to decide: the families of a font stack, the sections of a home screen, the files of an attachment, a queue. For a long list, offer a way to sort it instead.
- Reorder your items in the `move` handler, or as soon after it as your framework renders. You may move the elements you have or render new ones: the list finds the moved item by its place and its name.
- Give each item a `label` when its content's text does not name it well: the name is part of its handle's name, so the handles of two items are told apart, and it is what the announcements say.
- Write each sentence whole, with its places. The list builds no phrase of its own, so the order of the words is your language's.
- A control of yours in an item (a button that removes it) keeps its own focus stop, after the handle. Name it with the item ("Remove Inter"). When you remove the item that holds focus, the list moves focus to the handle of the item that takes its place; say the removal yourself.

### Choosing a selection mode

| Selection | The list is | Choosing an option |
| --- | --- | --- |
| None (default) | A list | Nothing is chosen. Interactive items emit their own click. |
| Single | A listbox | Selects the option and clears the one that was selected. It cannot be cleared by choosing it again. |
| Multiple | A multi-select listbox | Switches the option on or off. |

The arrow keys only move focus; the selection changes when the person presses Space or Enter, or taps the item. A selected option is drawn with the selected fill and a check at its end, and the check's space is kept in every option, so the content lines up.

### Do

- Give a selectable list a `label` that says what the options are, and a reorderable list one that says what the items are.
- Give an item an `href` when it goes to a page, and keep `interactive` for an item that acts in place.
- Put a heading between two lists to make sections. The list has no section header of its own.

### Don't

- Don't put a button, link, switch or any other control inside an interactive item, a link item or an option. The item is one element, so assistive technology cannot reach a control inside it, and a press on the control also activates the item.
- Don't use an option for something that runs at once. A listbox chooses a value.
- Don't draw your own drag handle or move buttons: `reorderable` draws the handle, and its menu is the way to move that needs no dragging.

## Accessibility

<ComponentAccessibility name="list" />

### What the component handles

- A list without selection is exposed as a list, and each item as a list item with its position and the list's size. An interactive item is one button inside its list item, and a link item is one link there. A selectable list is a listbox named by `label`, with its orientation; each item is an option with its selected state.
- A list with interactive items, links or options is one Tab stop. The arrow keys of the arrangement move between items without wrapping (Up and Down in a column, Left and Right in a line, mirrored in a right-to-left layout; in a grid, across the lines too). Home, End and typeahead work. Static items take no focus; a control in them keeps its own Tab stop.
- In a reorderable list each handle is a menu button named by your `dragHandleLabel` with the item's name, described by your `dragHandleHint`, and a Tab stop of its own. Its menu moves the item with single presses, which meets WCAG's requirement that whatever is done by dragging can be done without it (2.5.7). Space lifts the item for the keyboard (2.1.1).
- One polite live region says when the keyboard lifts an item, where it would land at each step, where it stands once a move took effect, and that it went back after Escape.
- A dragged item follows the pointer with the dragged fill and shadow; the others slide aside over the reorder motion. Where motion is reduced, everything but the item under the pointer moves at once. In forced colors a dragged item is outlined in the highlight color.
- Escape puts a dragged item back and does not also close a dialog the list is in. A long press on an item opens no menu of the browser's.
- An interactive item, a link or an option is at least the minimum target, its focus ring is drawn inside it, and it grows with larger text.
- Debug builds report an item without a name, a selectable or reorderable list without a label, an option whose value is empty or repeated, a selection that names no option, a control inside an interactive item or an option, a word nobody gave, and `reorderable` on a selectable list.

Known platform gaps:

- SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native ship the earlier list and none of this revision: no orientation, wrapping, gap, content slot or reordering.

### What you need to do

- Give a selectable or reorderable list a `label`.
- Give a reorderable list its words in your product's language. Without them the handles work unnamed and nothing is announced.
- Reorder your items when `move` fires. The list has changed nothing.
- Give a link item whose `target` is `new` a `targetLabel`, such as "opens in a new tab".
- Keep interactive items, links and options free of controls. An item that needs its own control stays static.
- For long data, build your own lazy list and state the set size where the platform needs it. The list does not scroll or virtualize.
