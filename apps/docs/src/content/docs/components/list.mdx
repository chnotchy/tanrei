### Usage

A list has one kind of item, `list-item`. Each platform takes the items the way it takes any repeated children. The web uses child elements, SwiftUI views in a view builder, Compose calls in a content lambda and Flutter widgets in `children`. React Native takes an `items` array of plain objects. The selection belongs to the list: `value` names the chosen option of a single-choice list, and `values` the chosen options of a multiple-choice list.

A row that goes to a page takes an `href` and is a link, not a button. It is drawn exactly as an interactive row, and the browser treats it as any link: it can be opened in a new tab, its address copied, and a crawler follows it. Setting `target` to `new` opens it in a new tab and marks the row with the icon set's `open_in_new`; `targetLabel` is then what is read after the row's name. Link rows are on the web today. SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native are lagging on them, and their samples show rows without a destination.

Setting `reorder` to `buttons` lets people put the items in another order. Every row draws two icon buttons: one moves it up by one place, the other moves it down. They are Tanrei Buttons, so a keyboard, a pointer, touch, a switch, voice control and a screen reader all use them the same way, and nothing has to be dragged.

The order is your data, so the list never changes it. A press fires `move` with `from`, the item's index, and `to`, the index it is to have, both counted from 0. Reorder your items in answer. The list then draws the two rows changing place, keeps focus on the same button of the moved row, and tells assistive technology where the row now stands. If you change nothing, nothing happens: that is how a product refuses a move.

Answer within the time of `comp.list.motion.reorder-duration`. An order that arrives later is still drawn, but the list has already taken the move as refused and does not announce it.

The three words are yours. `moveUpLabel` and `moveDownLabel` name the buttons, each with the place `{headline}` that the list fills with its row's headline (`Move {headline} up`), and `movedText` is the sentence that is announced after a move. Write the whole sentence in your language and mark three places, which the list fills: `{headline}`, `{position}` (counted from 1) and `{count}`. Give the words on the list, or once for the product with `setWords()`.

A reorderable list holds static rows only and is never a listbox. A row may hold one control of yours in its trailing slot, after the move buttons: typically a button that removes the item.

The `drag` value of `reorder`, which adds a handle that a pointer or a finger drags, is in the spec and not built yet. On the web it draws the two buttons and no handle. Reordering is on the web today. SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native are lagging on it.

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a list for a column of things of one kind: messages, people, files, settings, or the choices of a picker. Each row starts with the same kind of element and ends with the same kind of value, so the column can be scanned. Use a card for one thing with more to say, a table when people compare values across columns, and a menu for a short set of actions that opens from a button.

The list draws nothing of its own. It adds no background, no padding around its items and no scrolling. Put it on a surface, in a card (with `inset` off for a grouped list, as in iOS settings), or in your screen's scroll view.

### The four kinds of row

A row is always a `list-item`. What it is follows from the list's `selection` and the item's `interactive` and `href`.

| Kind | How you get it | What it is | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Static | `selection` none, `interactive` false | A list item that shows content. It takes no focus and draws no hover or press. | Rows people only read, and rows that hold their own control in the trailing slot, such as a switch. |
| Interactive | `interactive` true | One button inside its list item, named by the headline. | Rows that each change something or open it in place: a setting, a message in a pane, a dialog. |
| Link | `href` set | One link inside its list item, named by the headline and drawn exactly as an interactive row. Enter activates it and Space does not. `href` wins over `interactive`. | Rows that each go to a page with an address of its own: an index of pages, search results, related documents. |
| Option | `selection` single or multiple | An option of a listbox, with a selected state. | Choosing from the rows: a theme, a language, the labels of an issue. |

A list may mix static, interactive and link rows. A selectable list holds only options: `interactive` and `href` are ignored there. A reorderable list holds only static rows: `interactive` and `href` are ignored there too, because a row that is itself one button or one link cannot hold the buttons that move it.

### Lists people reorder

- Use `reorder` for a short list whose order means something and is the person's to decide: the families of a font stack, the sections of a home screen, a queue. For a long list, offer a way to sort it instead. One press moves a row one place.
- Reorder your items in the `move` handler, or as soon after it as your framework renders. You may move the elements you have or render new ones: the list finds the moved row by its place and its headline.
- Write the move buttons' words with the row's headline in them (`Move {headline} up`, `Move {headline} down`). The headline is part of each button's name, so the buttons of two rows are told apart in a list of the page's buttons and by voice control.
- Write `movedText` as one whole sentence with the three places. The list builds no phrase of its own, so the order of the words is your language's.
- Put at most one control in the trailing slot, and name it with the item ("Remove Inter"), so that the remove buttons of two rows are told apart.
- The list announces a move and nothing else. When you remove or add an item, what is said is yours. When you remove the last item, move focus yourself, to where a person adds one.
- Give the rows room. Where the move buttons and the trailing control would leave the text narrower than `reorder.text-min-width` (96 px at the default text size, and larger as the text is enlarged), every row of the list places them on a line of their own below the text, and the trailing control on a further line where that one is too short. In the default system a row with a remove button and no icon, avatar or image keeps them beside its text where the row is about 300 px wide or wider at the default text size; at 200% text they stand below it.

### Choosing a selection mode

| Selection | The list is | Choosing an option |
| --- | --- | --- |
| None (default) | A list | Nothing is chosen. Interactive rows emit their own click. |
| Single | A listbox | Selects the option and clears the one that was selected. The selected option cannot be cleared by choosing it again, as with radio buttons. If "none" is a valid answer, add an option for it. |
| Multiple | A multi-select listbox | Switches the option on or off. |

The arrow keys only move focus. The selection changes when the person presses Space or Enter, or taps the row. This is deliberate: in a list, choosing a row can start something slow, and moving through fifty rows must not start fifty loads.

A selected option is drawn with the selected fill and a check at its end. In the default system the fill is a layer of the row's own content color, as a menu's checked item is, so it is told from a hovered row on any surface. The check's space is kept in every option, so the text lines up. The check is what meets the 3:1 contrast for the state. The fill alone does not.

### Rows and their parts

- A headline alone gives a one-line row. An overline, or a line of supporting text, gives a two-line row. Two or more lines of supporting text give a three-line row. Rows grow past these heights with their content and with larger text.
- `supportingMaxLines` cuts long supporting text with an ellipsis, for a preview column. The full text is still read to assistive technology.
- At the start of a row, use a leading icon, or leading media: an avatar (a round box, for people) or an image (a larger rounded square, for things). Media replaces the icon.
- At the end, use a short trailing text (a count, a time), a trailing icon (a chevron for a row that opens something), and on a static row your own control. A link row whose `target` has the value `new` draws `open_in_new` as its trailing icon by itself, unless you name another.
- Icons are names of glyphs in the design system's icon set (`wifi`, `chevron_right`). A glyph that shows a direction along the line is mirrored in a right-to-left layout.
- `divider` draws a hairline under the row, starting where the text starts, in the one hairline color every component uses. Set it on every row but the last, or at the end of a group. You do not add a [divider](/components/divider/) of your own between rows.

### Do

- Keep the headline short and put details in the supporting text. The headline is the row's name.
- Give a selectable list a `label` that says what the options are, such as "Theme".
- Make a row that switches a setting a static row with a switch in its trailing slot. Name the switch by the headline and hide its visible label.
- Put a heading between two lists to make sections. The list has no section header of its own.
- Give a row an `href` when it goes to a page, and keep `interactive` for a row that acts in place. A row that navigates from a click handler is not a link: it cannot be opened in a new tab, and its address cannot be copied.

### Don't

- Don't put a button, link, switch or any other control inside an interactive row, a link row or an option. The row is one element, so assistive technology cannot reach a control inside it, and a press on the control also activates the row.
- Don't use an option for something that runs at once. A listbox chooses a value. Use interactive rows for actions.
- Don't disable a static row. It has no action to disable, so `disabled` is ignored there.
- Don't draw a checkbox or radio button in a row to show selection. Use `selection`, which draws the check and exposes the state.

## Accessibility

<ComponentAccessibility name="list" />

### What the component handles

- A list without selection is exposed as a list, and each row as a list item with its position and the list's size. An interactive row is one button inside its list item, and a link row is one link there.
- A link row is in the list's arrow-key order like an interactive row. Enter follows it; Space does what it does on any link and shows no pressed state. One that opens a new tab reads its `targetLabel` after its name and description. A disabled link row has nothing to follow or copy and is still reached with the arrow keys.
- A selectable list is exposed as a listbox named by `label`, multi-selectable while `selection` is multiple. Each row is an option with its selected state, its position and the list's size.
- An interactive row, a link row or an option is named by its headline and described by its overline, supporting text and trailing text, in reading order. Icons, the check, the divider and the leading media are hidden.
- A list with interactive rows or options is one Tab stop. Tab goes to the row focused last, else the first selected option, else the first enabled row. Up and Down Arrow move between rows without wrapping. Home and End go to the first and last row. Typing moves to the next row whose headline begins with the typed letters. Space and Enter activate. Static rows take no focus. A control in a static row keeps its own Tab stop.
- In a reorderable list each move button is a button named by `moveUpLabel` or `moveDownLabel` with its row's headline in the place `{headline}`. Each is a Tab stop of its own in reading order, before the row's trailing control. The list is not one Tab stop there, and the arrow keys do nothing. The first row's move-up button and the last row's move-down button are disabled and still drawn, so every row has the same parts in the same places.
- After a move, focus stays on the same button of the moved row, so pressing the key again moves the row again. Where the row has become the first or the last, focus goes to its other move button.
- A reorderable list has one polite live region. After a move it holds `movedText` with the row's headline, its new position and the number of rows, and a later move replaces it. The numbers are written as the list's language writes them. Nothing else announces a move.
- Two rows change places with a short motion, and at once where motion is reduced.
- When you remove the row that holds focus, focus goes to the same control of the row that now stands in its place, else of the row before it.
- Where the controls would squeeze the text, they move to a line of their own below it, and no control gets smaller than its target.
- Disabled rows can still be reached with the arrow keys, so people learn they exist, but they cannot be activated.
- The focus ring is drawn inside the row, so a scroll view around the list cannot clip it. It shows for keyboard focus only.
- An interactive row or an option is at least the minimum target tall, and the whole row is its hit area. Rows never overlap each other's hit areas.
- Rows grow with larger text. The headline wraps and is never cut.
- Debug builds report a row without a headline, a selectable list without a label, an option whose value is empty or repeated, a selection that names no option, an option that has an `href`, an `href` that is a `javascript:` address or cannot be read, an unknown `target`, and a link row whose `target` has the value `new` and that has no `targetLabel`. For a reorderable list they report a move word nobody gave (once per word), text between braces in `movedText` that is not one of the three places, a row that has `interactive` or an `href`, and `reorder` on a selectable list.

Known platform gaps:

- Web: the `drag` value of `reorder` is not built. It draws the two move buttons and no handle, and nothing can be dragged.
- SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native have no reorderable list yet: no `reorder`, no `move` and none of its three words.
- SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native have no link rows yet: no `href`, `target` or `targetLabel`.
- SwiftUI has no list or listbox role and no multi-selectable property. The list is a container named by `label`, each interactive row or option is a button (an option has the selected trait while chosen), and each carries its position as its value ("2 of 3"), in English only.
- SwiftUI never gives keyboard focus to a disabled button, so the arrow keys pass over a disabled row instead of reaching it.
- SwiftUI always hides the leading media from assistive technology, so a photo you name is not read.
- SwiftUI reads an option's value only from a `.tag(_:)` applied to the `TanreiListItem` itself, before any other modifier. A tag on another view does not reach it.
- SwiftUI: the arrow keys, Home, End, typeahead and the Tab stop are tested as rules, not yet with a real keyboard.
- React Native has no listbox role and no multi-selectable property. A selectable list keeps the list role, and each option has the option role and its selected state. What VoiceOver and TalkBack announce for the list itself is up to the platform.
- React Native has no position-in-set property. An interactive row or an option carries its position as its value text ("2 of 3"). A static row is not an element of its own, so no position is announced for it. The position text is in English unless you pass `positionText`.
- React Native: the arrow keys, Home, End and typeahead arrive through a key event that has not yet been checked on a device with a hardware keyboard.
- Flutter has no listbox or option role, no multi-selectable flag and no position or set size. A selectable list is exposed as a list of selectable list items.
- Flutter always hides the leading media from assistive technology, so a photo you name is not read.
- Compose has no list, listbox or option role and no multi-selectable flag. The list is a collection, and TalkBack announces each option as selected or not.
- Compose: inside a `LazyColumn` in the list, only the rows that are composed count toward the position, the set size and the arrow keys.
- Compose always hides the leading media from assistive technology, so a photo you name is not read.
- Compose: only the list's Tab stop accepts a focus request. A call to focus another row, from your code or from an accessibility service, does nothing.
- Compose: the keyboard through the platform's real key handling, and TalkBack on a listbox, have been tested under Robolectric only, not yet on a device.
- SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native cannot inspect what you put in a row's slots, so they do not report a control inside an interactive row or an option. The rule is yours to keep.

### What you need to do

- Give a selectable list a `label`. Without one, the listbox has no name.
- Write headlines that tell rows apart. The headline is what a screen reader says first, and what typeahead matches.
- Give a link row whose `target` has the value `new` a `targetLabel` in your product's language, such as "opens in a new tab". Tanrei ships no words of its own.
- Give a reorderable list its three words in your product's language: `moveUpLabel`, `moveDownLabel` and `movedText`. Without the first two the buttons work and have no name. Without the third a move is not announced.
- Reorder your items when `move` fires. The list has changed nothing, and an order you give later than the reorder motion's time is drawn without an announcement.
- Say a removal or an addition yourself, and move focus yourself when the last item is removed.
- Keep interactive rows, link rows and options free of controls. A row that needs its own control stays static and holds it in the trailing slot, with the control named by the headline.
- If an avatar or image carries meaning that the headline does not, say it in the supporting text. The media is hidden from assistive technology unless you name it, and SwiftUI, Compose and Flutter never read it.
- For long data, build your own lazy list and state the set size where the platform needs it. The list does not scroll or virtualize.
- On React Native, pass `positionText` in your product's language. On SwiftUI the position is read in English, and there is no way to change it yet.
