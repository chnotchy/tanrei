## Overview

<ComponentAvailability name="switch" />

:::note[A revised spec, not yet on every platform]
This page describes the switch as the spec has it now: a 52 × 32 track, a handle that grows under a press, glyphs only from the icon set, a handle that follows a drag, and handle glyphs with a heavier weight of their own. The web component already does all of this, and so do the demos. SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native are marked lagging above: until they catch up they ship the previous revision, which answers a tap only and follows no drag.
:::

The handle is a circle in every condition, with or without a glyph. A tap switches it, and the handle slides to the other end while its size and every color that differs between off and on change with it. A drag along the track carries the handle with the finger and draws the same in-between looks, with no threshold. The switch changes when the drag is released: to the side the handle is nearer to, or the side a fling goes toward. A drag that is cancelled, by Escape or by the platform, changes nothing.

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a switch for one setting that applies as soon as it changes: Wi-Fi, notifications for a conversation, a dark theme. The person sees the result without pressing a save button. When the choice only takes effect after the person confirms a form, use a checkbox instead.

### Checkbox, radio button or switch

| Control | Choices | Takes effect | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Checkbox](/components/checkbox/) | Any number of independent choices, including none | When the person confirms | Options in a form, agreeing to terms, selecting rows |
| [Radio button](/components/radio-button/) | Exactly one from a small group, all shown | When the person confirms, usually | One answer to one question |
| Switch | One setting, on or off | At once | Settings that apply as soon as they change |

### Icons in the handle

`checkedIcon` and `uncheckedIcon` name a glyph in the system's icon set for the handle, which makes the two positions easier to tell apart at a glance. A check for on is the common choice; leave `uncheckedIcon` empty to mark only the on position. The icons are decoration: the state is announced from the switch itself, never from the glyph.

The glyph is always the icon set's, drawn in the family and fill of the switch's position. It has a weight of its own, heavier than the system's other icons (500 in the default system, where the icons are 300), because at the handle's size a stroke of the icons' weight is under one pixel wide. Its size does not change, and a preset keeps its own icon weight. The switch draws no check or cross of its own; a product that wants a different mark chooses a different icon set. While the icon font is still loading, the handle keeps the size it has with a glyph and shows nothing in its place, never the glyph's name as text. The glyph appears once the font can draw it.

### Size and press

In the default system the switch has a 52 × 32 track with a 2 outline, and a handle of 16 while off and 24 while on or with a glyph. A press grows the handle at once to fill the track, in either position. Once the press ends it returns to its size over the state motion, or at once where motion is reduced. A system that answers a press with nothing keeps the handle's size.

### The same thumb as tabs

The switch and [tabs](/components/tabs/) share one model: a thumb on a track that slides to what is chosen and follows a finger. A switch has two states that are not equals, so its track, outline and handle differ between off and on. Tabs offer two or more equal choices, so their track never changes. Use a switch for a setting that is on or off, and tabs to choose among views.

### Read-only

A read-only switch shows a setting that the person can read but not change, such as one held by a policy or one only an administrator may change. It stays in the focus order, is announced with its state and as read-only, and is submitted with its form. It looks like an enabled switch without the hover and press feedback, and its handle cannot be dragged. Say near it why it cannot be changed. Use `disabled` when the setting does not apply at all.

### Do

- Name the setting in the label, not the action: "Wi-Fi", not "Turn on Wi-Fi".
- Apply the change as soon as the switch moves. If the setting cannot be applied, move the switch back and say why in text next to it.
- In a list row whose headline already names the setting, hide the label with `labelHidden` and keep it as the accessible name.

### Don't

- Don't use a switch in a form that is submitted later. Use a checkbox.
- Don't use a switch to choose between two options that are not on and off, such as "List" and "Grid". Use radio buttons.
- Don't change the label to "On" or "Off" to show the state. The handle's position shows it, and the platform announces it.

## Accessibility

<ComponentAccessibility name="switch" />

### What the component handles

- It exposes the switch role with its state (on or off) and its disabled state, never a checked checkbox where the platform tells the two apart.
- The label is the accessible name, whether it is drawn or hidden with `labelHidden`. The drawn text and the icons are hidden from assistive technology.
- A click or tap anywhere on the track or the label toggles it. The track and the label are the hit area, and the hit area meets the minimum target.
- A drag along the track carries the handle and switches on release. It is never the only way: a tap, a click on the label and Space all switch it. With a screen reader running, a one-finger drag belongs to the reader, and the switch is toggled by the reader's activation.
- During a drag the switch may look partly on, but its state, and the value it would submit with a form, stay as they were. The change is announced once, when the release makes it.
- Space toggles it. Enter toggles it only where the platform activates any focused control with Enter (Android, tvOS); on the web, Enter submits the form.
- While `readOnly` is set it stays focusable and is announced as read-only beside its state. No tap, drag, Space, click on the label or `click()` changes it, and it shows no hover or press feedback. On the web its value is still submitted. HTML's `readonly` does not apply to the switch's native element, so the component does this itself.
- In a right-to-left layout, the handle's positions, its travel and the label are mirrored. A drag still carries the handle to where the pointer is.
- It shows the focus indicator for keyboard focus only.
- A missing label is reported in debug builds.

Known platform gaps:

- SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native ship the previous revision: the switch answers a tap and follows no drag, and its glyphs are not yet drawn at the mark's own weight.
- `readOnly` is on the web only so far: SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native do not have it yet.

### What you need to do

- Write a label that names the setting, and keep it the same whether the switch is on or off.
- When a change fails, or applies somewhere the person cannot see, tell them in text. The switch does not announce anything beyond its own state.
