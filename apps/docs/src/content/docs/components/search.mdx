### Usage

The search is a bar and a view. The bar holds a search glyph, the input, a clear button while there is a query, and up to two of your own icon buttons at the end, such as voice input or the account. Your own menu or back button can take the glyph's place in the `leading` slot. The view opens while the person searches and holds, from top to bottom, the suggestions, a status row, the results, and then any content of your own.

**A bar or a trigger.** `bar` says what stands where the search is placed:

- `field` (default): the bar with its input.
- `button`: a trigger shaped like the bar that holds no input, only the search glyph, the placeholder and the shortcut hint. Use it in a header that has room.
- `icon`: an icon-only button that shows the search glyph. Use it in a header that has none.

A trigger opens a modal view, a dialog or the full-screen view, and the input is in that view's header. One product uses `button` on a wide window and `icon` on a narrow one.

**Three presentations.** `presentation` says where the view opens:

- `docked` (default): a surface just under the bar, as wide as it, in the platform's overlay layer, as a menu opens under its anchor. Focus stays in the bar's input, so the person keeps typing while suggestions change. If there is not enough room below, the view opens above the bar.
- `dialog`: a modal surface near the top of the window, over a scrim, with a header that holds a back button, the input and the clear button. It is for a large window, opened from a trigger. A press on the scrim closes it.
- `full-screen`: a modal view over the whole window, with the same header. It takes focus, keeps it, and returns it to the bar or the trigger when it closes.

Use docked or the dialog on large windows and full-screen on small ones. The search does not choose for you: set `presentation` from your own breakpoints. A trigger with `docked` opens the dialog, because a docked view has no input of its own, and debug builds report it.

**Suggestions** are an item kind. Each one has a `value`, a `headline`, and optionally `supportingText` and a `leadingIcon`, such as history for a past query or a folder for a result. You supply the suggestions for the current query; the search draws them and does not filter them. Choosing one emits `select` with its value. The search never changes the query itself, so you decide what a choice means: fill in the query and search, or open the result.

**Results** are a second item kind, for the things the search found: a page, a section, a message. A result has a `value`, a `headline`, an `excerpt` and optionally a `leadingIcon`, and it goes somewhere: with an `href` its row is a real link, which the platform opens as it opens any link — in a new tab with a modifier key, with its own context menu. Without an `href` the row is a button, and `select` is the only signal. A **result group** puts results under one heading: a quiet title such as "Messages", or, with an `href` of its own, the page the results are sections of. The excerpt is a passage in which you mark what matched; the search does not look for the query in the text. On the web the excerpt is the element's own content with `<mark>` around the matches, or the `excerpt` runs, every second one a match.

**Status.** The search never runs a query, waits or filters: it emits `input` for every change and draws what you give it. Tell it what your search is doing with `status` (`idle`, `loading` or `error`) and `statusText`: the count ("12 results for tokens"), that nothing was found, or the failure. The status row shows the text and announces it when it changes, and while loading it shows a loading indicator after a short delay.

**The shortcut.** `shortcut` names the keys that open the search, in the [keyboard shortcut](/components/keyboard-shortcut/)'s key names (`mod+k`, `/`). The search writes them as a hint, a line of text for the person's keyboard: at the end of a button trigger, and in the bar while it is empty and not focused. It exposes them as the keyboard shortcut of the trigger or the input. The keys work only if you bind them, or set `bindShortcut`: the search then listens in the whole window while it is shown and enabled, opens on the keys and closes on them again.

**Opening and closing.** A press on the bar, typing, or Down Arrow in the input opens the view. Focus alone never opens it: tabbing onto the bar, or focus returning from the full-screen view, leaves it closed. The search asks to open or close with `toggle`, which carries the new state and, when closing, the reason: `escape`, `back`, `select`, `submit`, `outside`, `tab`, `focus-out` or `shortcut`. The query stays as it is when the view closes. Enter in the input emits `submit`; the view then stays open while it shows results or a status, so that the person can go on to them. On the web the element sets its own `expanded`, and on SwiftUI and Flutter the binding or controller changes. On Compose and React Native you set `expanded` from the callback, as for the menu.

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use the search when searching is a main way into the product's content, such as mail, files, contacts or settings, and suggestions help people get there faster. Put it at the top of the screen or of the page it searches.

Use it as a site search too: a trigger in the header that opens a dialog, with the pages and sections found as links.

To filter a short list that is already on screen, or to enter a search term in a form, a [text field](/components/text-field/) with `type` search is enough. Choosing one value from a fixed list is a job for a [menu](/components/menu/) or a list, not a search.

### The bar or a trigger

| `bar` | What stands in the page | Opens | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `field` (default) | The bar with its input | Any presentation | A product where search is a main way in, and the bar has a place of its own |
| `button` | A trigger shaped like the bar: the glyph, the placeholder and the shortcut hint | A modal view: the dialog or full-screen | A header with room, where search is one of several things |
| `icon` | An icon-only button with the search glyph | A modal view: the dialog or full-screen | A header without room, such as a narrow window |

A trigger is named by `label`, and a button trigger shows the placeholder, or the label where there is none. When its view closes, focus returns to the trigger.

### Docked, dialog or full-screen

| Presentation | Where the view opens | Focus | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Docked (default) | Under the bar, as wide as it, over the content | Stays in the bar's input. Nothing else becomes inert, and there is no scrim | Large windows, where the view can open without hiding the whole screen |
| Dialog | Near the top of the window, centred, up to a maximum width, over a scrim. Its top stays put while its content changes | Moves to the header's input, stays in the view, and comes back to the trigger or the bar when it closes | Large windows, opened from a trigger: a site search |
| Full-screen | Over the whole window, with its own header, back button and divider | Moves to the header's input, stays in the view, and comes back to the bar or the trigger when it closes | Small windows, where suggestions and the on-screen keyboard leave no room for anything else |

The query is the same in all three. Switching `presentation` at a breakpoint does not change it.

### The clear and back buttons

Both are built in, because their behaviour belongs to the search: clear empties the query and keeps the view and focus where they are, and back closes the full-screen view. Their names must be in your product's language, so you give them: `clearLabel` ("Clear search") and, for the dialog and full-screen, `backLabel` ("Back"). On iOS the back button is the only way out of the full-screen view. The dialog's header has the back button too, so a person with neither Escape nor a pointer can leave it.

### Suggestions, results and your content

- **A suggestion is something to choose; a result is somewhere to go.** A suggestion helps finish the query or jumps to a known thing, and the arrow keys make it active while focus stays in the input. A result is a thing your search found: a link that takes focus itself.
- Use the leading icon to tell kinds apart: history for a past query, a folder, a page, a section.
- Keep headlines short and put detail in the supporting text or the excerpt: "Invoices", then "Folder · 24 messages". They wrap rather than being cut off.
- Mark in the excerpt what matched. The mark is the system's, a color, a heavier weight and a quiet fill behind the match, so matches look the same everywhere and are found in every script, also where the font has no weight between regular and bold.
- Group results when they belong together: sections under their page, messages under "Messages". A group's heading is a link when the group itself is a place.
- Give a result an `href` whenever it has an address. A person can then open it in a new tab, copy its address and see where it leads. Use `select` without an `href` only for a result your product opens itself.
- The view's order is fixed: suggestions, the status row, results, then your own content, such as a "Load more results" button or a link to a full results page.
- A docked view with nothing to show draws nothing, and the search stays open, so it appears again as soon as there is something.

### Status

- Set `statusText` once for each answer: "12 results for tokens", "No results for tokne". It is announced when it changes, so changing it on every keystroke would be noise.
- Set `status` to `loading` while a query runs. The loading indicator appears only after a short delay, so a fast answer shows none.
- Set `status` to `error` with a text that says what happened. It is drawn as an error and announced at once.
- Drop answers that arrive out of order. The search draws whatever you set last.

### The shortcut

- Use a shortcut people already know for search in your kind of product, and write it with `mod` so that it is right on every keyboard.
- Set `bindShortcut` on one search of a screen. Of two searches that bind the same keys, the one shown first keeps them, and debug builds report the other.
- A shortcut without a modifier, such as `/`, is ignored while focus is in a text input, so that the person can type the character.
- If you bind a single character key with no modifier, such as `/`, give people a way to turn it off or to change it (WCAG 2.1.4, Character Key Shortcuts). `bindShortcut` is off by default.
- Without `bindShortcut` the hint only shows the keys: bind them yourself, or leave `shortcut` empty.

### Do

- Write a placeholder that says what can be searched: "Search mail". Set `label` too: the placeholder is never the name.
- Update the suggestions as the query changes, and keep them in a stable order while the person moves through them.
- On `select`, do what the suggestion promises: open a result, or put a query into the bar and run it.
- Keep the trailing slot to two icon buttons at most.
- Give `targetLabel` ("opens in a new tab") to a result whose `target` is `new`.

### Don't

- Don't open the view when the bar merely receives focus. The search already avoids this; don't add it back yourself.
- Don't put controls that need focus of their own, such as a text field, in a docked view. A press on the docked view leaves focus in the input.
- Don't change the query when a suggestion becomes active. Only a choice (`select`) or the person's typing changes it.
- Don't use a search to choose a value in a form.
- Don't put a result's address only in `select`. A row with no `href` is a button, and a person cannot open it in a new tab.
- Don't mark matches yourself with color alone, or put markup of unknown origin in an excerpt. On the web the excerpt's content is yours: what you place there is shown as it is.

## Accessibility

### What the component handles

- The input is a search field named by `label`, never by the placeholder, with the platform's search role where it has one.
- On the web, the docked view follows the WAI-ARIA combobox pattern. While suggestions show, the input is a combobox with `aria-autocomplete="list"`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls` naming the list, and `aria-activedescendant` naming the active suggestion. The suggestions are a listbox named by `label`, and each option is named by its headline and described by its supporting text.
- Focus in the docked view stays in the input. The active suggestion moves with Down and Up Arrow, so the person can keep refining the query. When an active suggestion is partly out of view, the view scrolls it in.
- The full-screen view and the dialog view are modal dialogs named by `label`. Focus moves to the header's input with the caret at the end of the query, stays inside the view, and everything outside becomes inert and hidden. When the view closes, focus returns to the bar's input or to the trigger, and the view does not open again.
- A trigger is a button named by `label` that says it opens a dialog and whether its view is open. It shows keyboard focus as the bar does.
- A press on the dialog's scrim closes it and reaches nothing under it. Its header starts with the back button, which Tab reaches.
- Results are a list named by `label`, and a group is named by its headline. A result with an `href` is a link, never an option; the input is a combobox only while the view shows suggestions. A result is named by its headline and described by its excerpt, then by `targetLabel` when it opens in a new context.
- Down Arrow in the input, with no suggestion left to go to, moves focus to the first result and on through them, across groups; Up Arrow comes back, to the input from the first. Typing, deleting or pasting on a result moves focus to the input, where the key then acts. In a modal view Tab goes through the results as through any links.
- The status row is a live region. `statusText` is announced when it changes, and at once as an alert while `status` is `error`. While loading, the results are marked busy and the loading indicator is named by `statusText`.
- The shortcut is exposed as the keyboard shortcut of the trigger or the input. Its hint takes no press and no focus.
- Focus reaching the input never opens the view, whether by Tab or by returning from the full-screen view.
- Escape, the platform's back action and the back button close the view. The docked view also closes on Tab, Shift+Tab, a press outside, or focus leaving the search.
- The clear and back buttons are Tanrei icon buttons named by `clearLabel` and `backLabel`. After clear, focus returns to the input.
- Your icon buttons in the `leading` and `trailing` slots keep their own names, roles and focus stops, in reading order. They are not shown in the full-screen header.
- Typing, input-method composition, dictation and right-to-left text work as in the text field. The search keyboard's action key submits.
- The bar shows focus with its own edge, whether focus came from the keyboard or a pointer, as a field does. Suggestion rows show the focus ring inside the row while they are active. Each row and the bar meet the minimum target.
- The search glyph, the leading icons and the divider are hidden from assistive technology.
- At larger text sizes the bar and the header grow, the query stays on one line, and suggestion rows wrap.
- Debug builds report a missing `label`, `clearLabel` or `backLabel`, an empty or repeated suggestion value, an empty headline, an unknown presentation, bar or status, a trigger with the docked presentation, a second search that binds the same keys, and more than two trailing buttons.

Known platform gaps:

- SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native do not have the trigger, the dialog view, results, the status row or the shortcut yet. On those platforms, show results in the view's own content and announce their number yourself.
- Web: a bound shortcut reads `mod` in the convention its hint is written in, the one an ancestor sets or else the platform's, where the spec names the platform's alone.
- SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native have no combobox or listbox role. Their input is a search field, and each suggestion is a button with its position in the list, which a screen reader reaches by swiping. The active suggestion is not announced as the input's active descendant there. Flutter's suggestions have no position in the list either, and SwiftUI and React Native state the position in English ("2 of 3"; React Native's `positionText` changes it).
- Flutter exposes the input as a search input type, not the search box role, because Flutter 3.32, the oldest version it supports, rejects that role.
- A press outside the docked view closes it. On the web, SwiftUI, Compose and Flutter the press also reaches what is under it; on React Native it does not, as with the menu.
- Web: the full-screen header's input is a combobox too, so the active suggestion is announced there as well. The spec names the combobox only for the docked view.
- Web: the docked view does not close on the platform's close request, such as Android's back gesture. It is placed by script, not with CSS anchor positioning as the spec's web note says.
- Disabled reaches the slots' icon buttons, which the spec leaves in their own state: SwiftUI's `.disabled(true)` disables them too, and on the web a disabled opacity below 1 would fade them. Every shipped system's disabled opacity is 1.
- SwiftUI draws the docked view above what the search's own container holds after it, not above every view of the window. Keep the search late in its container, or raise it with `zIndex`. On macOS the window's height is not read, so the view always opens below at its natural height, and the full-screen view is a sheet as large as the window.
- SwiftUI: the selection highlight follows the caret's tint, not the text field's selection color. The first tap on an unfocused input puts the caret at the end of the query, not where the tap landed. The keys are tested on macOS only, not with a hardware keyboard on iOS.
- Compose cannot tell a fine pointer from a touch, so its suggestion rows are always 48 tall where the web's are 44 under a mouse. A press outside the docked view is not reached by its tests.
- Flutter: a docked view the product opens with `expand()` while the input has no focus is not closed by a press outside. The full-screen view needs a `Navigator`; without one the view is docked.
- React Native: the docked view is placed when it opens and when the window or the keyboard changes, but does not follow the bar while it scrolls. Escape, the arrow keys and Tab arrive only where `TextInput` delivers them, and the overlay and the full-screen view's focus moves have not yet been checked on a device.
- Under `cupertino`, the bar draws no focus edge, as an iOS search field shows focus only by its caret and the keyboard.
- Under `m3-like`, the search draws the default system's values: Material's search values wait for the fidelity reference to cover it. Material's docked view that grows out of the bar as one surface is drawn as a separate surface under it (mimicry gap `search-docked-view-joins-bar`).

### What you need to do

- Give the search a `label`, and give `clearLabel` and, for the dialog and full-screen, `backLabel`, in your product's language.
- Supply suggestions for the current query, with headlines that make sense read on their own.
- Say what your search found in `statusText`, in your product's language, once for each answer. The search announces it; it does not count results itself.
- Run the query yourself, and drop answers that arrive out of order.
- Give every result that has an address an `href`, and a `targetLabel` when it opens in a new context.
- Bind the shortcut's keys, or set `bindShortcut`. A hint for keys that do nothing misleads.
- Decide what `select` does, and set the query yourself if a choice should fill it in.
- On Compose and React Native, set `expanded` from the callback, or the view will not open or close.
- On the web, give the search a `name` to submit its query with a form.
