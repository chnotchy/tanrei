A card comes in three forms. A plain card is a container: its content keeps its own semantics and may hold buttons and links. An interactive card is one button. A link card, a card with `href`, is one link. Use a link card when activating the card takes the person to another page or address, and an interactive card when something happens where they are.

### Usage

The Web and React samples show the current spec. SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native are lagging: they ship the plain and the interactive card, and no link card yet. Their samples show what they ship.

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a card to group content about one subject, such as a report, a person, a product or a setting, and set it apart from what is around it. The card only insets, clips and colors its content. You lay the content out with Tanrei's text styles (`heading-5` for a card's title) and components.

Make the whole card interactive when it is one action that opens what it shows. If it needs several actions, keep it non-interactive and put buttons inside.

### A card with a destination is a link

A control with a destination is a link, and that holds for a card as well. When activating the card only takes the person to another page, give it `href` instead of handling a click on an interactive card. The card is then a real link, on the web an anchor around its content. People can open it in a new tab, copy its address and see where it leads before they follow it, and assistive technology announces a link, not a button. The cards that lead from one page of this site to another are link cards.

- A link card looks exactly like an interactive card in every state. There is no link look and no visited state.
- `href` wins over `interactive`: a card with both is a link.
- Enter follows a link card. Space does not: it does what it does on any link, which on the web is to scroll the page.
- `click` fires first, then the destination opens. Cancel the click to take the navigation yourself, as a client-side router does.
- `target` is `same` (the default) or `new`. A link that opens a new tab or window needs `targetLabel`, the words read after its name, such as "opens in a new tab". Tanrei ships no words of its own. The card draws no mark for it, because the content is yours: put the `open_in_new` glyph in the content if you want to show one.
- A disabled link card cannot be followed by any means and leaves the focus order.
- An `href` of only white space is no destination. A `javascript:` address, or one that cannot be read as an address, is refused and reported in debug builds, and the card is what it would be without it.

When a card needs a destination and its own actions as well, keep it plain and make its heading a link, or put a [Button](/components/button/) with `href` inside.

### Choosing a variant

Choose by the background, not by importance.

| Variant | Separates by | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outlined (default) | An edge | Most cards, and cards on a surface of the same tone as a filled card. |
| Filled | A tone | Cards on a plain surface, where the tone alone marks the group. A filled card disappears on a surface of the same tone. |
| Elevated | Depth | Content that sits above the page, such as a pinned or draggable item. |

### Do

- Name an interactive card or a link card with `label`, normally the text of its heading.
- Use one variant for a set of cards shown together.
- Use `inset` false (`flush` on the web) for media that bleeds to the edges, and pad the text below it yourself with the card's padding token.

### Don't

- Don't put a button, link, field, toggle or anything else focusable in an interactive card or a link card. Assistive technology cannot reach it, and a press on it would also activate the card.
- Don't navigate from the click handler of an interactive card. A card that goes somewhere takes `href`.
- Don't disable a plain card. `disabled` is ignored there, because there is no action or link to disable.
- Don't use elevation to rank cards by importance.

## Accessibility

<ComponentAccessibility name="card" />

### What the component handles

- A card that is not interactive has no role and leaves its content's semantics alone. With a `label` it is a group named by it.
- An interactive card is one focusable element with the button role, and exposes its disabled state. Its name is `label`, or, without one, its whole content read as one string. Debug builds report a missing label.
- A link card is one focusable element with the link role, named the same way. While `target` is `new`, `targetLabel` is its description, read after its name and role. A disabled link card keeps the link role, exposes the disabled state and has no destination.
- Enter and Space activate an interactive card, and Enter alone a link card. The hit area meets the minimum target, and the focus indicator shows for keyboard focus only.
- It grows with its content, so large text never clips.
- On the web, debug builds report an interactive element inside an interactive card or a link card on every slot change, and a link card that opens a new tab without a `targetLabel`.

Known platform gaps:

- SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native have no link card yet. There, a card that goes somewhere is still an interactive card that navigates from its action.
- Web: an interactive element added later, deeper inside an element already in the card, is not reported.
- Web: a card that is not interactive never produces a click of its own, but a click on its content is an ordinary DOM click and bubbles through the card.
- SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native cannot detect a nested interactive element, so they do not check for one. The rule is yours to keep.
- React Native: a non-interactive card with a label is a group, but iOS maps that role to no trait. Whether VoiceOver announces the group's name has not been verified on a device.
- Compose: activation by Enter and Space through the platform's key handling is tested under Robolectric only, not yet on a device.
- React Native has no inherited text color. The card draws a plain string in its content color; your own text reads it with `useContentColor()`.
- SwiftUI: an interactive card smaller than the minimum target keeps its visible size, but takes up the target's size in the layout.

### What you need to do

- Give an interactive card or a link card a `label`. Without it, the whole content is read as one long name.
- Give a link card that opens a new tab or window a `targetLabel` in the language of your product.
- Keep an interactive card and a link card free of other controls. When a card needs its own buttons or links, keep it plain and let one of them be its main action or destination.
- Give headings, images and other content inside a non-interactive card their own semantics: alt text, heading levels. The card does not add any.
