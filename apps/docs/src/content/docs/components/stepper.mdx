A stepper sits above, or beside, the content of a flow that has a position: a checkout, a set-up, an application in several parts. One step is current, the steps before it are complete, one may need the person's attention, and the rest are still to come.

### Usage

The steps are `<tanrei-stepper-step>` children, in order. `value` names the current step. The stepper never moves `value` itself: when the person presses a step it fires `select` with that step's value, and your flow goes there (or doesn't) and sets `value`.

SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native are planned: the names beside each platform at the top of this page are the ones the spec gives them, and nothing ships there yet.

The words are yours: `completed-text` and `error-text` are what assistive technology hears after a step's label, and `position-text` is the compact form's line, with the places `{position}` and `{count}`. Give them once for every stepper with `setWords({ stepCompletedText, stepErrorText, stepPositionText })`.

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a stepper when a person goes through a flow of three to seven steps in an order the product sets, and knowing where they are, and how much is left, helps them finish: a checkout, a sign-up with several parts, an application. Show it above the step's content (horizontal), or beside it in a column (vertical).

Don't use a stepper:

- for instructions a reader works through on their own. That is [Steps](/components/steps/): a numbered procedure with no position, whose markers are plain rings and never show progress.
- for a flow of two steps, or one so short that the position tells nothing. A heading that says what this page is for is enough.
- for a long list of independent tasks done in any order. That is a task list: one row per task with its status, each a link.
- in place of the flow's own navigation. Keep the Back and Continue buttons. A stepper says where the person is; it is not how most people move.

### Conditions

| Condition | What it means | How it is drawn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current | Where the person is. | A filled marker with the step's number. |
| Complete | Done. By default every step before the current one. | A marker with a check. |
| Error | Needs the person's attention, wherever it stands. | A ring and a priority glyph, and the label in the error color. |
| Incomplete | Still to come. By default every step after the current one. | A ring with the step's number. |

`status` on a step is `auto` by default: complete before the current step, incomplete after it. Set it when your flow knows more: `error` for a step that needs fixing, `complete` for a step done out of order, `incomplete` for a step that was skipped and still has to be done. Each condition differs by its marker's shape, not by color alone.

When the flow is finished, leave `value` empty and mark every step `complete`.

### Going back to a step

- `pressable="visited"` lets the person press the steps they have been to — complete or with an error — to go back. Most flows want this.
- `pressable="all"` lets them press any step that isn't current, for a flow whose steps can be done in any order.
- `pressable="none"` (the default) only shows the position.

A step that can be pressed looks the same as one that can't until it is hovered or focused, as in most systems. If your users need more of a hint, say so near the stepper ("Select a step to change it"). The current step is never pressable. A step that can't be gone to yet (it depends on an earlier choice) is `disabled`: it is drawn faded and announced as unavailable.

### Narrow screens

A horizontal stepper turns compact by itself when its width can't hold every step at its least width: a bar of segments filled up to the current step, the position in your words ("Step 2 of 5"), and the current step's label. `layout="compact"` asks for that form at any width. The compact form can't be pressed: the flow's Back button does that job. `layout="vertical"` keeps every step visible in a column instead.

### Do

- Name steps by what the person does or gives, in a word or two: Account, Shipping, Payment, Review.
- Keep the number of steps the same from start to finish. If a step only sometimes applies, show it and skip it, rather than adding it halfway.
- Put the error on the step where the person can fix it, with a short description of what is wrong.

### Don't

- Don't use the stepper as the only way to move forward.
- Don't write numbers into the labels. The marker shows the number and the list announces it.
- Don't use a stepper for a page's sections or for tabs. Those are [Tabs](/components/tabs/) or a table of contents.

## Accessibility

<ComponentAccessibility name="stepper" />

### What the component handles

- The stepper is an ordered list named by its `label`. Each step is an item read as its label, then the word for its condition (`completed-text`, `error-text`), then its description. The list gives each step its place ("3 of 4"), so the number in the marker isn't read again.
- The current step is exposed as the current step (`aria-current="step"`).
- A pressable step is a native button and a Tab stop, in the order of the steps. Enter and Space press it. A step that can't be pressed is plain text and not a stop. A disabled step of a pressable stepper is a disabled button.
- Markers, glyphs, connectors and the bar are hidden from assistive technology.
- Conditions differ by shape as well as color: a filled marker, a check, a priority glyph, a ring.
- In the compact form the stepper is one group named by its label, reading what is drawn: the position and the current step's label, and the error word where the current step has one.
- Labels and descriptions wrap and are never cut. At larger text sizes everything grows, and the stepper still turns compact by the width it has.
- Under forced colors the current marker takes the highlight color and everything else the text color.
- Debug builds report a missing label, a value that names no step, an empty or repeated step value, a step with no label, a stepper with fewer than two steps, a current step marked disabled, a position sentence without `{position}`, and a missing word.

### What you need to do

- Give the stepper a `label` that says which flow it tracks ("Checkout progress").
- Give `completed-text`, `error-text` and `position-text` in your language, or once with `setWords()`.
- Keep the flow's own Back and Continue buttons, and move focus to the new step's heading when the step changes, so a screen reader user hears where they are.
- When a step has an error, show the error itself in the step's content too. The stepper only says that the step needs attention.
