### Usage

A toolbar holds a set of related controls: actions, toggles, a button that opens a menu, a [button with menu](/components/button-with-menu/). Its items are your own Tanrei [Buttons](/components/button/) and buttons with menu, each with its own label, click and state. The toolbar lays them out in reading order, sets groups apart, moves keyboard focus among them and hides what does not fit into an overflow menu. It never restyles them, so an icon-only Button in a toolbar looks and behaves as it does anywhere else.

It has six kinds of item:

- `action`: an icon-only Button. A toggle such as Bold is one with `toggle` set.
- `button`: a Button with a label, which may be a toggle too, such as a filter that is on or off.
- `menu-button`: a Button with a Tanrei [Menu](/components/menu/) anchored to it, such as Sort or Insert. The menu is yours and stands beside its Button. The toolbar does not lay it out.
- `button-with-menu`: a main action with a menu of related ones. Each half is its own stop of the arrow keys.
- `group`: a run of related items, such as Bold, Italic and Underline. It draws nothing of its own. Give it a `label` when the grouping means something to the person, such as "Text style".
- `separator`: a line between two items that are not in groups. On the web it is a [divider](/components/divider/), which the toolbar draws as it draws the line between two groups.

**Inline (default).** An inline toolbar stands where your layout puts it, in the content it acts on: above a table, at the top of an editor, beside a list as a row of filters. It is as long as its items need and never longer than the layout allows. In the default system it has no surface, shadow or edge of its own, and its ends line up with the content around it.

**Docked.** A docked toolbar spans the bottom edge of the screen. Its color reaches under the home indicator or gesture bar, and its items stay above it and inside the side insets. The web reads `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, SwiftUI places the bar with `.safeAreaInset(edge: .bottom)`, Compose takes `windowInsets` (the navigation bars by default), and Flutter reads the bottom `MediaQuery` padding. React Native takes its insets from the toolbar's `insets` prop or from `TanreiProvider`, since Tanrei takes no safe-area dependency.

**Floating.** A floating toolbar does not place itself, as a [main action button](/components/main-action-button/) does not. Your layout puts it, normally centered above the bottom of the content, or beside the content's end edge when it is vertical.

The Web and React samples show the current spec. SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native are lagging. They still ship the previous revision of the toolbar and of the button, where a toolbar is docked unless you say otherwise, holds only Buttons, scrolls what does not fit, and an icon-only action is a separate icon button. Their samples show that revision until they catch up.

## Guidelines

### When to use

Use a toolbar for a set of controls that act on what is in front of the person: Bold, Italic and Link above an editor; Filter, Sort and Download above a table; Archive, Delete and Move for an open message; a pen and an eraser over a drawing; zoom and location on a map. The controls act on the current content or selection, not on where the person goes next.

For the screen's title, the way back and one or two global actions, use the [app bar](/components/app-bar/) at the top. To move between the top-level destinations of an app, use the [navigation bar](/components/navigation-bar/). A docked toolbar and a navigation bar never share a screen: both claim the bottom edge.

### Inline, docked or floating

| Variant | Where it sits | Items | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inline (default) | In place in the content, where your layout puts it, with no surface of its own in the default system | Closed up, as long as they need, each keeping room for its own touch target | The controls of a part of the page: an editor's formatting, a table's actions, a row of filters |
| Docked | Across the bottom edge, flush with it, its color reaching under the system bar | Spread across the width, with equal space between | The actions of a detail screen on a phone: an open message, a photo, a document |
| Floating | A rounded, raised surface over the content, placed by your layout | Closed up, as long as they need, each keeping room for its own touch target | Tools over content that should stay visible around them: a canvas, a map |

A docked toolbar is always horizontal. An inline or a floating one may be vertical, beside the content. One product can place a toolbar inline on one screen, dock one on another and float one on a third. A variant that a design system adds takes the inline structure with its own values.

A docked toolbar spreads its items across the width and never brings them closer than its gap (16 in the default system). An inline or a floating toolbar closes its items up: two neighbours stand apart only as far as each needs to keep a touch target of its own along the row, and never closer than the gap (4 inline and 0 floating in the default system). Large buttons touch, medium ones end up 8 apart and small ones 16. The toolbar never widens or restyles an item to do this, and the spacing is the same for a mouse and for touch.

### Items

- Use icon-only [Buttons](/components/button/) of the `text` variant for most actions. Give the one action the toolbar leads to, such as Send or Done, more weight: a filled icon-only button, perhaps at the `wide` width, or a filled button with a label when a word reads better than an icon.
- A `narrow` icon-only button suits a toolbar with many small actions. It is drawn narrower than its target, and the toolbar keeps room for the target between its neighbours.
- Make an action that switches something on and off (Bold, Mute) an icon-only button with `toggle`. A filter that is on or off is a labelled button with `toggle`. Each keeps its own selected state and its own announcement.
- For a control that offers a choice of several commands, anchor a [menu](/components/menu/) to a Button in the toolbar. For a main action with variations, such as Save and Save as, use a [button with menu](/components/button-with-menu/).
- Keep the order stable. People find actions by position, and a toolbar whose items move between screens is hard to learn.
- A docked toolbar with one item centers it. Consider whether that item belongs in the content instead.

### Groups and separators

Put related items in a group: Bold, Italic and Underline; Undo and Redo; Zoom in and Zoom out. The toolbar sets two groups apart, and a group from an item outside any group, the way the design system sets parts apart elsewhere: in the default system with a short line and 8 of space on each side, or, under a design system whose separation is space, with 16 of empty space and no line. Set `separation` on a toolbar only for a reason of its own. A separator item draws the same line between two items that are not in groups.

The toolbar never draws a line at either end, and never two side by side. Give a group a `label` when the grouping means something to the person ("Text style"), so that a screen reader names it. Without a label, the group sets its items apart and says nothing.

### When the items do not fit

The toolbar never wraps or shrinks an item. When its items need more room than it has, it hides items from the end, one at a time, and shows an overflow button after the last item that remains. The button opens a menu of the hidden items, in their order, with their labels, icons and states: an action is a menu item, a toggle a checkbox item, a menu button a submenu, and a button with menu its main action followed by a submenu. Choosing one does exactly what the hidden item does. Name the overflow button with `overflow-label` ("More actions"), or once for the product with `setWords({ toolbarOverflowLabel: … })`.

Mark the item that must stay, such as Send or Done, with `toolbar-overflow="never"`: the toolbar hides the items before it instead. If what is never hidden still does not fit, the rest of the row scrolls. To keep every item in the row and scroll them instead of hiding any, set `overflow="scroll"`.

What is hidden is decided before the toolbar is drawn, and again in the frame in which its length changes, so an item is never drawn and then hidden, and the row never jumps.

### Do

- Name the toolbar for what its controls do: "Formatting", "Message actions".
- Disable an action that cannot apply right now rather than removing it, so the others do not move.
- Order the items so that the ones people need most come first: the toolbar hides from the end.
- Place a floating toolbar clear of the content people need to see while they use it, and clear of a navigation bar and the system inset.

### Don't

- Don't put a text field, a slider or a set of chips in a toolbar. Each one needs the arrow keys that the toolbar uses to move focus. Search belongs in the app bar or the page.
- Don't use a docked toolbar and a navigation bar on the same screen.
- Don't restyle the items to make the toolbar look emphasized. Choose the tonal or filled variant for the buttons instead.
- Don't put navigation destinations in a toolbar. Its items are controls that act on the content.

## Accessibility

<ComponentAccessibility name="toolbar" />

### What the component handles

- The toolbar is one Tab stop, following the WAI-ARIA toolbar pattern. Tab enters it at the item that had keyboard focus last, or else at the first item that is not disabled, and the next Tab leaves it. A formatting toolbar of eight toggles costs one Tab press to cross, not eight.
- Inside, the arrow keys along its axis move to the next or previous item and wrap at the ends: Right and Left Arrow in a horizontal toolbar, Down and Up Arrow in a vertical one. Home and End move to the first and last item. In a right-to-left layout, Left Arrow moves to the next item. A group's items are the toolbar's items, each half of a button with menu is a stop of its own, separators are skipped, and the overflow button is the last stop.
- Moving focus never activates an item. Enter and Space act on the focused item as that item does, so a toggle still switches.
- While a menu anchored to an item is open, the keys are the menu's. When it closes, focus returns to the item it opened from.
- When the item that has focus is hidden because the toolbar got narrower, focus moves to the overflow button.
- Disabled items are skipped by the arrow keys and are never the Tab stop, because a disabled button is not focusable.
- The roving focus is the same one the list, menu and tabs use on each platform.
- The toolbar is exposed as a toolbar named by `label`, with its orientation, where the platform has a toolbar role (the web, React Native). SwiftUI, Compose and Flutter have none: there it is a group named by `label` that contains its items in order.
- A group with a `label` is exposed as a group with that name. Lines between groups are exposed as separators.
- The overflow button is exposed as a button that opens a menu, with its expanded state, and its menu holds every hidden item with its own name and state.
- Each item keeps its own name, role, state and target. The toolbar adds nothing to them and never restyles them.
- Between two items the hit areas never overlap. In a docked toolbar they meet at the middle of the gap, and the default system's gap (16) is wide enough that every item keeps its whole 48 touch target there too; a design system with a narrower gap narrows the targets between items. In an inline or a floating toolbar the items stand just far enough apart for each to keep a touch target's length of its own along the row, whatever the pointer, so a narrow or a small button is never squeezed by its neighbours. On a side with no neighbour, an item keeps its whole target.
- When `overflow` is `scroll` and an item that receives keyboard focus is scrolled out of view, the toolbar scrolls it fully into view.
- At larger text sizes the toolbar grows across its axis to hold its items. It does not scale them.
- Debug builds report a missing `label`, a toolbar with no items, an item that is none of its kinds, a docked toolbar set to vertical, a missing name for the overflow button, and an unknown variant, orientation, overflow or separation.

Known platform gaps:

- SwiftUI, Compose and Flutter have no toolbar role. A screen reader hears a named group, not a toolbar, and each package's README says so. The group does not expose its orientation.
- SwiftUI, Compose, Flutter and React Native are lagging: they have no inline variant, no groups or separators, no overflow menu, and they take only Buttons as items.
- Web: a docked toolbar reads `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, which is zero unless the page's viewport meta has `viewport-fit=cover`.
- Web: the overflow menu of a vertical toolbar opens below the overflow button, not beside it, because the menu has no side placement.
- Web: an inline or a floating toolbar measures the room it may take from its parent element. A toolbar whose room is set by its siblings in a flex row, not by its parent, decides what fits only when its parent or its items change size.
- Web: while the items scroll, whatever of an item reaches past the toolbar across its axis is clipped: its hit area, its focus ring or glow, an elevated button's shadow. No scrollbar is shown.
- On SwiftUI the one Tab stop is a focus group for the keyboard: Full Keyboard Access moves among its items with the arrow keys and leaves it with Tab. The keys are tested as rules, not yet with a real keyboard.
- SwiftUI: while the items scroll, an end item's hit area stops at the toolbar's edge. When a resize makes the items start or stop scrolling, they are recreated, so an item loses its keyboard focus or a press in progress.
- On React Native the Tab stop and the arrow keys work only where a hardware keyboard is attached, as in the tabs row.
- React Native has the toolbar role but no way to expose an orientation, so a vertical toolbar is not announced as vertical.
- React Native: the floating toolbar's width, the keys on a hardware keyboard, scrolling a focused item into view, and whether VoiceOver and TalkBack announce the toolbar have been checked in Jest only, not on a device.
- Under `m3-like`, the toolbar draws the default system's values: Material's toolbar values wait for the fidelity reference to cover it. Material's vibrant toolbar, which recolors its items, cannot be drawn, because the toolbar never restyles its items (mimicry gap `toolbar-vibrant-color`).

### What you need to do

- Give the toolbar a `label`, every item its own label in your product's language, including icon-only actions, and the overflow button a name with `overflow-label` or your product's words.
- Put only Tanrei Buttons and buttons with menu in it, alone, in groups, or with menus anchored to them.
- When an action cannot apply, disable it, and say why nearby when the reason is not obvious.
- Open and close the menu of a menu button yourself, as you do for any menu.
- Place a floating toolbar so that it does not cover content that is still needed while the toolbar is open.
