A callout is a notice in the flow of the page: it stands where you put it, as wide as its place, for as long as the content does. It is a note by default, read where it stands like the paragraph before it, and it never takes focus.

On the web it is `<tanrei-callout>`. The other platforms have not implemented it yet.

### Usage

The body is the element's own content. The title is the `headline` attribute, or the `title` slot where it is your own content or a heading. A glyph of the icon set goes in the `icon` slot, and your own Buttons or links in the `actions` slot.

<Tabs syncKey="platform">
  <TabItem label="Web">
    ```html
    <!-- A note. Its status is in its title's words. -->
    <tanrei-callout status="info">
      <p slot="title">Note</p>
      Text styles are named by level, never by size.
    </tanrei-callout>

    <!-- The title does not say the status: status-label is read first ("Warning, …") and not drawn. -->
    <tanrei-callout status="warning" status-label="Warning" headline="Accessibility">
      A preset is held to its original, not to these contrast thresholds.
    </tanrei-callout>

    <!-- A tip: no status, your glyph, the word in the title. -->
    <tanrei-callout headline="Tip">

    </tanrei-callout>

    <!-- Long-form content is prose, at the callout's size, with no measure. -->
    <tanrei-callout size="large" status="warning">
      <h3 slot="title">Back up before you migrate</h3>

        <p>The migration rewrites the database in place. <a href="#restore">See how to restore a copy</a>.</p>
        <ul><li>…</li></ul>
      </div>
    ```

## Guidelines

### Snackbar, callout, dialog

The three are told apart once, by where they stand and how long they stay:

| Kind | What it is | Where it stands | How long it stays | Announced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Snackbar](../snackbar/) | The result of something the app just did or that just happened. | Above the content, at the window's edge. | Briefly, or until it is closed. | Always. |
| Callout | A notice that is part of the content. | In the flow, where you put it. | As long as the content does. | Only when it appears in answer to something (`live`). |
| [Dialog](../dialog/) | A question the person must answer before going on. | Above everything, with a scrim. | Until it is answered. | By taking focus. |

A **banner** — a notice for a whole page or screen, above its content — is not a fourth thing. It is a callout that you place above that region's content, usually `live`, usually with an action or a close button. Put it where it comes first in reading order for the region it concerns.

A message under one field (this value is not a date) is not a callout either: it is the field's own supporting text.

### Choosing a look

| `variant` | Draws | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `tonal` (default) | A quiet fill. | Most callouts. |
| `outlined` | No fill and one hairline. | A callout on a tinted surface or inside a filled card, where a fill would vanish, and many callouts close together. |

The hairline is the system's one hairline in every status. A status never colors it.

### Statuses

`status` is `default`, `info`, `warning`, `error` or `success`: the names the snackbar and the tag use, for what they mean everywhere in the system. A status draws its colors and its glyph (`info`, `warning`, `error`, `check_circle`), and decides how a live callout is announced. It changes nothing else.

| What you are writing | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A note: something worth knowing | `status="info"` |
| A caution: something that needs attention | `status="warning"` |
| A danger: something that will go wrong | `status="error"` |
| A tip: advice the reader may take | `status="default"`, the glyph `lightbulb` in the `icon` slot, and the title "Tip" |

A tip is not a success: nothing succeeded. The `icon` slot takes any glyph of the icon set, with or without a status, so "experimental" and "new in this version" need no new value either. The word goes in the title.

### Sizes

| `size` | Title | Body | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `medium` (default) | 14 on a line height of 20 | 14 on 22 | A callout among controls: a form, a panel, a dialog. |
| `large` | 16 on 24 | 16 on 26 | A callout in a page whose purpose is the text. |

These are prose's two sizes. Only the text styles change: the paddings, the glyph and the close button are the same.

### Title

`headline` is plain text and never a heading. Most titles are labels of a note, and a page whose outline lists "Note" five times is worse for it. Where a callout opens a part of the page that people navigate to, put a heading of the level your outline needs in the `title` slot. The callout draws it as any title and changes neither its role nor its level.

On a page that is rendered on a server, give the title in the `title` slot: a `headline` attribute is drawn by the element, so it is not shown before the element is defined.

### Body

Text placed directly in the element takes the callout's text style and color. Long-form blocks (paragraphs, lists, a table, a code block) go in a prose of the callout's size without a measure:

```html
<tanrei-callout status="info">
  <div class="tanrei-prose" data-measure="false">…rendered Markdown…</div>
</tanrei-callout>
```

Prose brings no color, so it reads on every fill, and its links are underlined. A component placed in the body keeps its own styles. A callout in a prose that keeps a measure is kept to the measure like a text block; everywhere else it fills the width it is given.

### Actions and closing

The actions are your own [Buttons](../button/) or links, one or two. Use `outlined` or `text` Buttons: a `tonal` Button has the fill of the tonal callout without a status and cannot be seen as a button there. The callout lays them out in a row that wraps, and does nothing else: it does not close when one is pressed.

`closable` draws a close button: a small icon-only [Button](../button/), named by `close-label`, in the variant `--tanrei-comp-callout-close-button-variant` names (tonal in the default system). On a tonal callout it is drawn in the callout's own text color — a translucent circle of that color with the glyph in it — so that it shows on the fill of every status; on an outlined callout, which has no fill, it is the Button as it is anywhere else. It answers hover, focus and a press as every Button does. Pressing it sets `open` to false and fires `dismiss`. The callout never closes by itself and does not answer Escape. When a person closes one, remember it: a closable callout that returns on every visit is noise. A caution that qualifies the content beside it should not be closable.

### Appearing in answer to something

`live` is for a callout that appears or changes while the person works: a summary of a form's errors after a submit, a notice that the file changed underneath them. Keep it in the page with `open="false"` and set `open` when it applies.

  <tanrei-callout id="callout-demo-live" live open="false" status="error" headline="Three fields need attention" closable close-label="Close" style="inline-size: min(100%, 34rem)">Enter a name, choose a start date, and give an address that can receive mail.</tanrei-callout>

```html
<tanrei-callout id="errors" live open="false" status="error" headline="Three fields need attention">
  Enter a name, choose a start date, and give an address that can receive mail.
</tanrei-callout>

<script type="module">
  form.addEventListener('submit', (event) => {
    if (!form.checkValidity()) {
      event.preventDefault();
      document.querySelector('#errors').open = true; // announced once, assertively
    }
  });
</script>
```

A live callout is announced once when it is shown and when its title, its body or its status changes: politely for `default`, `info` and `success`, and assertively for `warning` and `error`. One that is in the page's markup when the page loads is not announced then.

On the web, a live callout that your script adds to the page after it has loaded is announced. The element cannot tell a callout added in answer to something from one added with a new view of a single-page application, so for a notice that is simply part of a new view, leave `live` off.

### Before the element is defined

The body, the title slot and the actions are the element's own children, so they are in the document: found by search in the page, translated, selected, and read without script. `@tanrei/web/callout.css` gives a callout that is not defined yet the box it will have — its paddings, its text styles, the room of its glyph and of its close button, and nothing at all for `open="false"` — so nothing moves when the element is defined. Until then it shows without the fill, the glyph and the close button.

### Do

- Say the status in words. "Warning: back up first" needs nothing more; "Accessibility" on a warning needs `status-label="Warning"`.
- Use the statuses for what they mean everywhere. Advice is not a success.
- Put one list of points in one callout rather than several callouts in a row.

### Don't

- Don't use a live callout for a note that is written into the page. Static content is not an alert.
- Don't nest callouts, and don't stack several at the top of a page: notices of the same weight side by side cancel each other.
- Don't both move focus to an error summary and announce it. Moving focus to the summary suits a long form whose first error may be off screen; announcing it and moving focus to the first field in error suits a short one.
- Don't rely on the color to carry the status. In forced colors every callout is an edge and text in the text color.

## Accessibility

<ComponentAccessibility name="callout" />

### What the component handles

- It is a note (`role="note"`), named by its title, or by `status-label` where it has no title. With `live` it is a live region instead: `role="status"`, or `role="alert"` for a warning or an error.
- `status-label` is read before the title and the body as a phrase of its own ("Warning, …") and is never drawn. It is ignored while `status` is `default`.
- The glyph is hidden from assistive technology.
- A title given as text is plain text. A heading in the `title` slot keeps its level.
- It is not a stop in the focus order and never moves focus. Tab reaches the links and controls of the body, then the actions, then the close button.
- When it is closed while focus is inside it, focus returns to the element that had it before it entered the callout, so the next Tab reaches what followed.
- The close button is a Button: its hit area reaches the minimum target, and on a tonal callout its glyph and its focus ring are in the callout's text color, which keeps at least 6:1 against the button in every status and color scheme of the default system.
- With enlarged text everything wraps and grows; the glyph and the close button stay on the first line.
- In forced colors every look gains an edge in the text color, and the glyph is drawn whole.
- In a right-to-left layout the glyph moves to the right and the close button to the left, a glyph the icon set marks is mirrored, and a left-to-right title keeps its own order.
- An unknown variant, size or status, a callout with no title and no body, a status that is not given in words, a close button without a name and a `status-label` without a status are reported in debug builds.

### What you need to do

- Check that each callout's words say its status. The component cannot tell whether a title does.
- Write `status-label` and `close-label` in the language of the page.
- Use `live` only for a callout that appears or changes in answer to something.
- Give an action a label that fits. A Button's label is one line, so on a narrow screen with enlarged text a long label can be wider than the callout.
