Every modal surface has the same layer under it. It dims the window behind the surface, so the page reads as set aside. It makes everything there inert: nothing behind can be pressed, focused or read by a screen reader, and the page does not scroll. It moves focus into the surface, keeps it there while the surface is open, and gives it back when the surface closes. And it asks to close when the person presses Escape, uses the platform's back, or presses the dim.

The modal layer is that layer, as a component of its own. The [dialog](/components/dialog/), the [side sheet](/components/side-sheet/) and the [bottom sheet](/components/bottom-sheet/) when they are modal, the modal [navigation drawer](/components/navigation-drawer/), the [image preview](/components/image-preview/), the modal [date-time picker](/components/date-time-picker/) and the [search](/components/search/)'s modal views are all built from it. Each of them follows its rules instead of having rules of its own.

It is an **advanced** component. You meet it in two ways:

- **To restyle every modal surface at once.** The layer's tokens, `comp.modal-layer.*`, are what each of those components' scrim and motion tokens point to. Set them in your product's layer and every modal surface changes together: the dim's color and opacity, a blur of what lies behind, and how long the layer takes to come and go.
- **To build a modal surface Tanrei does not have**: a command palette, a takeover of the whole window, a viewer of your own. Put your surface inside the layer and it behaves like every other modal surface of your product.

For a decision, use the dialog. For a panel, use the modal side sheet or bottom sheet. They already name themselves, place their actions and give a way out.

On the web it is `<tanrei-modal-layer>`. The other platforms have not implemented it yet.

### Usage

The layer is controlled. Set `open` to show it. When the person asks to close it, it emits `close` with the reason (`escape`, `back` or `scrim`) and stays open: you set `open` back to false. Give it a `label`, which is the name a screen reader announces.

The layer draws nothing of its own but the dim. Your content keeps its own color, shape and padding, and the layer centres it in the window at its own size. Place it elsewhere with the platform's own means (on the web, `::part(container)`).

### Restyle every modal surface

A product sets the layer's tokens in its layer file, and every modal surface follows:

```json
{
  "tokens": {
    "tanrei.comp.modal-layer.scrim.color": { "$value": "{tanrei.sys.color.scrim}" },
    "tanrei.comp.modal-layer.scrim.blur": { "$value": { "value": 8, "unit": "px" }, "reason": "Our modal surfaces frost what lies behind them." }
  }
}
```

The dim's opacity is the alpha of its color: change the color, or the system's `scrim` role, to make it lighter or darker. To set one surface apart, set that surface's own token instead (`comp.dialog.scrim.color`, `comp.dialog.motion.enter-duration`). It points to the layer's until you do.

## Guidelines

### What the layer does and what it leaves to you

| The layer | Your content |
| --- | --- |
| Dims the window behind it (or draws nothing, with `scrim` off), to every edge. | Its own color, shape, shadow and padding. The layer adds none. |
| Makes everything behind inert and keeps the page from scrolling. | Its own scrolling, when it is taller than the window. |
| Moves focus in, keeps it there, gives it back on closing. | Marks the element that should take focus first, where there is one. |
| Asks to close on Escape, the platform's back and a press on the dim. | A way out of its own: a Close, Cancel or Done button. |
| Centres the container in the window at its content's size. | Where it goes instead, if not the centre. |

### Without the dim

Turn `scrim` off for content that covers the whole window with a fill of its own, such as a viewer over a dark background. There is nothing left to dim. The layer still blocks everything behind, and a press outside your content still asks to close.

### Do

- Name the layer with `label`. A screen reader announces it when focus moves in.
- Put a visible way out in the content. Not everyone knows Escape, and back is not on every device.
- Change a product's modal surfaces through `comp.modal-layer.*`, so that they stay alike.

### Don't

- Don't build a dialog or a sheet from the bare layer. Use the dialog or the sheet, which already give a headline, actions and their places.
- Don't turn `dismissible` off without a way out in the content. The person would have no way to leave.
- Don't use it for something that should not block the page. A popover, a menu or a snackbar leave the page usable.

## Accessibility

<ComponentAccessibility name="modal-layer" />

### What the component handles

- The container is a modal dialog, named by `label`. Everything outside it is hidden from assistive technology while it is open.
- Focus moves into the layer when it opens, even when it is written open. It goes to the element you marked, or else to the container, so the name is read first.
- Tab and Shift+Tab wrap inside the layer. Focus never reaches the page behind.
- Closing gives focus back to where it was. If that element is gone, focus goes where the platform puts it.
- Escape asks to close only once everything inside has heard it. A field that clears its text on Escape, or a menu that closes, keeps the layer open.
- A layer opened from inside another stacks above it. Only the top one answers Escape, back and its dim.

### What you need to do

- Give a `label` that says what the surface is for.
- Mark the first element to focus when a form or a search field is the reason for the surface.
- Give the content a visible control that closes it, and keep `dismissible` on unless that control exists.
